# Mini Squash

_Cucurbita pepo_ · Fruiting plant

## At a glance

- **Thinning:** Keep 1 sprout per pod and cut the rest at the base.
- **Sprouts in:** 7–14 days
- **First harvest:** 3–4 months after planting (typical for its kind)
- **Matures in:** 50–75 days
- **Height:** under 2 ft
- **Light zone:** [High](/knowledge/plants/gardyn-light-zones/)
- **Full dose of plant food:** Once it has true leaves or roots 1/2 inch long
- **Care level:** Expert

## Harvest

Pick when a squash is about 2-3 inches across, roughly pool-ball size, with deep green skin and paler green streaks. Cut it from the plant with clean scissors. Expect 2-3 squash at a time per plant, depending on care. Blossoms are edible too, so clip them 1-2 inches below the flower base, taking only male flowers and leaving some to pollinate the females if you want fruit.

## Pruning

Cut off any leaves that yellow or brown. Top the main stem once it passes 2 feet so side branches fill in and the vine stays inside the light. As flowers fade, pinch off any without a fruit forming, just below the flower head, so the plant puts energy into its next flush of blooms. Some leaf stress while fruit ripens is normal.

## Support

The plant trails downward on its own. Add a trellis or plant ties once heavy fruit starts forming.

## Pollinating

The plant grows separate male and female flowers, and fruit needs both. Male flowers open first on thin, straight stems, while female flowers open later on a round, swollen base like a tiny fruit. Move pollen between them with a fingertip or small brush, and note that a stressed plant's female flowers shrivel and drop almost right away. A male flower stripped of petals keeps 1-2 days in a dry, airtight container in the fridge.

## Pests and disease

This plant is a heavy feeder. Use the bloom-and-fruit nutrient dose of 1 teaspoon per gallon of tank water. Its roots grow vigorously, so check and trim them twice a month. Watch for aphids and spider mites.
