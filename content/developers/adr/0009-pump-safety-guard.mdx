# ADR 0009: Pump safety guard and latched faults

**Status:** Accepted, amended 2026-11-14 (full duty by default, a brown-out guard, the current guard and its hal.toml-only mode, fast reads, fail-safes, and retried scheduled runs)
**Date:** 2026-10-30

## Context

The pump is the one actuator that can damage the hardware: running dry burns
the pump, and Gardyn's board browns out above ~30 % duty (superseded by the
2026-11-14 amendment: never measured on a Gardyn 3.0, and stock runs at 100 %). garden-of-eden's
issue tracker documents every way its scattered checks were bypassed (#108,
#109, #111, #114). [sleepypod](https://sleepypod.github.io/developers/hardware/) ADR-0022 established the model we want: detect,
fail safe by de-energising, stay off until a human acknowledges, auto-recovery
only as an explicit opt-in.

## Decision

`hal_core::safety::PumpGuard` is the only path to the pump driver.

1. **Duty clamp** to `limits.pump_max_duty` (default 30 %; superseded by the
   2026-11-14 amendment: default 100 %, the pin held high).
2. **Bounded runs** — every start has a deadline ≤ `pump_max_run_s` (default
   900 s). There is no unbounded "on".
3. **Dry-run interlock** — refuse to start when the median of N ultrasonic
   samples ≥ `water_low_cm`; **fail safe**: if the interlock is configured and
   the sensor is unavailable, refuse. (A running pump's level trip, and a
   second guard on the pump current: see the 2026-11-14 amendment.)
4. **Over-temperature** — refuse to start and stop a running pump at
   `over_temp_c` (PCB sensor, default 65 °C; raised from 36 °C on 2026-11-01
   after the stock Gardyn 3.0 board read 47 °C with the lights at 100 %).
5. **Latched faults** — a stop caused by (3) or (4) while running latches the
   pump off. `acknowledge` clears it; nothing else does. No auto-recovery.
6. **Stop always works**, latched or not.

The guard is pure and clock-injected; every rule has a unit test. `SimHal`
mirrors the same rules so the demo never shows behaviour the unit would refuse.

## Consequences

- Core, MCP, HA and the button cannot start the pump outside these rules.
- A dry tank produces a `pump_fault` alert plus `water_low`, and the UI/HA/MCP
  must acknowledge after refilling; this is deliberate friction. (Superseded by
  the 2026-11-14 amendment: a dry-run clears itself once the water reads good.)
- Dosing pumps (Phase 6.3) get their own guard with daily volume caps
  (ADR-0014).

## Amendment, 2026-11-14

Pump safety is decided in hald, from `hal.toml`, and outranks any setting from
the app. Everything below was decided or shipped between #231 and today; it is
part of #220 unless noted.

1. **Full duty by default (supersedes the 30 % clamp of Context and Decision 1).**
   `pump_max_duty` defaults to **100**. The 30 % figure came from
   garden-of-eden and was never measured on a Gardyn 3.0. The stock firmware
   drives GPIO24 plain on/off at 100 %, and 68 stock runs
   (`tests/fixtures/gardyn-gh30-stock-2024/pump_runs.csv`) drew a median of
   10.6 W for 5 to 15 minutes with no brown-out. The pump is a TD40A-1248A
   brushless DC pump with its own driver (12 V, 950 mA, 430 cm head); 50 Hz
   software PWM at 30 % starves that driver, and a community unit couldn't lift
   water to the top of the tower under seedypod though it could under stock.
   At duty 100 (and above, after the clamp) the driver holds the pin steady
   high with no software PWM (`hal_drivers::pwm::pin_drive`, `SoftPwm`); below
   100 it still pulses at 50 Hz. The cap stays configurable because pumps differ
   between units: the owner's lifts fine at 30 %. Lower it only for a board
   that browns out.
2. **Brown-out guard.** A new rule in `PumpGuard`. Past the spin-up grace (it
   holds the inrush), at least three reads in a row of the pump supply (the
   INA219's bus voltage, `pump_v`, read in the same transaction as the current
   every 100 ms) below `[limits] pump_brownout_v` (default **10.8 V**),
   covering `pump_brownout_ms` (default **300 ms**), stop the pump and latch
   `brown-out: supply fell to 10.2 V for 0.3 s`. 10.8 V is a 12 V supply 10 %
   low, the bottom of its usual tolerance. The supply idles at about 12.6 V, and
   a healthy one under the pump's 0.95 A reads about 12 V or more past the
   shunt, so the cutoff sits at least 1.2 V clear of normal running. The stock
   fixtures carry power, not voltage; their 10.6 W median, just under the
   pump's 11.4 W rating, is what a supply holding near 12 V delivers. A missing
   read neither counts nor clears the count, so it never trips the guard, and a
   sag that takes the monitor's reads with it isn't forgiven either.
   `pump_brownout_v = 0` turns it off; without an INA219 it is off. Where
   `/sys/devices/platform/soc/soc:firmware/get_throttled` is readable (no root
   needed), hald also reads the Pi's own under-voltage flag (bit 0) once a
   second while the pump runs, and two reads in a row that report it, at least
   `pump_brownout_ms` apart, trip the same fault (`brown-out: the Pi reported
under-voltage for 1 s`); `pump_brownout_pi = false` turns that off. A
   brown-out is checked before the current guard, because a sagging supply
   also draws less, and it waits for an acknowledge: it is not a dry-run.
   `hald --check` prints it (`pump_brownout_guard`), and `system.limits`
   reports `pumpBrownoutV`, `pumpBrownoutMs` and `pumpBrownoutPi`, which Water
   shows read-only beside the dry-run guard line.
3. **The current guard (#231).** A pump pulling air draws far less than one
   lifting water (stock: about 10.7 W wet, about 3.3 W once it has lost prime).
   Past a spin-up grace, current reads below `pump_dry_current_a` stop the pump
   and latch a dry-run that says what the pump drew. The cutoff ships unset,
   so the guard is off until wet and dry traces are recorded on a unit.
4. **Which guard runs is hal.toml's alone (#268).** `[limits] pump_dry_guard =
"level" | "current" | "both"`, default `both`. `current` or `both` without
   a cutoff or a pump monitor falls back to `level` with a warning, so the pump
   never runs with no dry-run guard it was asked for. The mode and the cutoff
   have no route in the app, the API or MCP.
5. **Faster reads and trips (#267).** While the pump runs, and for 2 s after,
   hald reads the pump current every 100 ms, skipping the INA219 facets' 500 ms
   cache; the ranger is read once a second. The INA219 averages 128 shunt
   samples and 16 bus samples (config `0x3E7F`), so one read spans about 3.4
   periods of the 50 Hz PWM and a conversion cycle (76.6 ms) fits inside the
   100 ms read. The current guard trips on at least three low reads covering
   `pump_dry_after_ms` (300 ms) after `pump_dry_grace_ms` (1 s). The level
   guard trips on trusted readings at the limit for 0.5 s (`LEVEL_CONFIRM`),
   which at 1 Hz is the second reading, about 1 s; with no trusted reading at
   all it still trips after 3 s (`DRY_CONFIRM`).
6. **The spin-up grace is the one guard setting the app may change (#268).**
   Core sends `pump/guard/set {dryGraceMs}`; hald clamps it into hal.toml's
   `pump_dry_grace_min_ms`–`pump_dry_grace_max_ms` (300–5000 ms by default,
   never past 10 s), keeps it in its own `state.json` and uses it at boot
   without waiting on core. The reason for letting the app tune this one: the
   worst outcome of a too-short grace on a slow-priming pump is not watering
   when there is water, and the owner should be able to fix that from Water
   without SSH.
7. **Fail-safes.** A stop the pump driver refuses latches `stop-failed: …` and
   is retried on every guard read until it takes (#267). In `current` mode,
   where the monitor is the only dry-run guard, no reading of the current for
   more than 2 s while running latches `monitor-lost: …` (#268). hald runs
   under systemd's watchdog (`Type=notify`, `WatchdogSec=10`), and every stop
   of hald, a crash or a watchdog kill included, runs `pump-off`
   (`ExecStopPost`), which drives the pump pin low with the pin hal.toml sets
   (#266).
8. **Latches.** Decision 5 holds for every fault above except a dry-run, which
   since 2026-11-10 clears itself once the water has read good for a whole
   window (`water_low`'s minute); a current trip holds that window from the
   trip, since a full tank says nothing about a pump pulling air. This
   supersedes "must acknowledge after refilling" in Consequences. `stop-failed`,
   `monitor-lost`, `over-temp` and `brown-out` still wait for a person.
9. **A refused scheduled run is retried (core, #270).** A pump schedule that
   hald refuses (for example on one failed ultrasonic read, which leaves the
   tank unknown and refuses a start by design) is retried once a minute, up to
   15 more times (`PUMP_RETRIES`), through the same `hal.setActuator` path, so
   hald's guard still decides every attempt. A start hald answers with the pump
   still off counts as refused, as an error does. Each run is recorded as
   `fired` or `refused: <reason>`. Retrying stops once an attempt is accepted,
   the schedule's next run is due, or the schedule is turned off; the retry
   state is in memory, so a core restart drops it. If every try is refused,
   core raises one `pump_schedule_missed` alert ("Scheduled watering at 08:00
   didn't run: …") and clears it when a scheduled run next fires. A weekly
   schedule no longer loses a week of water to one bad echo.
